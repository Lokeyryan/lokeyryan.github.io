Relic

The old, dirty fan with chipped paint
shouldn’t have caught my attention.
It had no right to,
planting itself there untouched at the antique fair,
among the cardboard boxes overflowing with
obscure 70s records
and globes with the U.S.S.R. still labeled.

Decades of motor oil,
dust, and dirt
formed a layer of grime
that coated the fan’s rusting once-teal base,
clotted on the once-gleaming blade, and
clung to the fan blade’s cage.
The fan used to oscillate,
but now,
after clicking its “on” switch,
it just shudders
with a memory
of its shiny past.

Yet I had to take it home.
it was a relic of a different age.
It didn’t oscillate, but
once it came off the factory lines
brand-new, durable, an item
built to last. That version
hid under the
layers of grime,
and I needed to
unbury it.
